# Study: LEGO Speed Champions 77244 — Mercedes-AMG F1 W15

Booklet 6566090 · 108 pages · 99 steps · 265 pieces (119 element types) · one
minifigure. Studied page by page; this note is the knowledge the F1 grammar
(`src/engine/assembly/f1.ts`) is built on. It's about **why** the car is built
the way it is, so the rules carry over to any open-wheel car, not just this one.

## 1. One architecture, many cars

The back of the booklet shows the 2024 range: 77242 Ferrari, 77245 Sauber,
77247 Aston Martin, 77248 Alpine, 77249 Williams, 77250 Haas, 77251 McLaren —
the same skeleton under different liveries. The design is a **template**:
fixed architecture, a few proportions, and a team palette (main, accent,
detail colours + stickers). That is the core idea encoded: a parametric
family, not a model.

## 2. Proportions (measured from the steps, in studs)

| Measure | Value | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall length | ≈ 21 (17 cm) | L |
| Monocoque (tub) width | 4 | the spine everything hangs on |
| Body width over sidepods | 8 | the Speed Champions "8-wide" standard |
| Track (wheel centres) | ≈ 9 | wheels stand **outside** the body on suspension |

| Height | ≈ 6 cm | body ≈ 2 bricks, airbox and rear wing up to ≈ 4 bricks |

## 3. Build order (bags 1–6)

1. **Floor and spine** (steps 1–12). A frame plate with an open centre
   (4656764) is the tub floor; Technic bricks with holes (1×4, 1×1) sit
   **in** the floor at the axle lines; teal plates under the floor are the
   plank (the underside is part of the livery).
2. **Rear suspension** (13–14): two identical sub-assemblies — axle pin,
   bush, a plate with holes, an *inverted* curved slope as the wishbone
   fairing, a 4L axle — slid into the rear Technic bricks.
3. **Cockpit sides and seat** (15–24): brackets turn studs sideways so the
   cockpit walls can be smooth tiles; red brackets carry the detail colour.
4. **Nose** (25–32): narrow (2 wide), stepping down to the front: wedges and
   a curved nose slope; the steering wheel goes in here.
5. **Engine cover and sidepods** (33–51): long curved slopes ("tunnel"
   shapes) along the spine; the sidepod/engine block (41–50) is built
   **sideways** in the hand on SNOT bricks (studs on two sides) and brackets,
   so both faces are flat panels for stickers, then pushed in (51).
6. **Front wing** (57–69): a separate sub-assembly — wedge plates fanning out,
   the nose tip on top, end plates of red brackets on green jumper plates —
   attached through one bar (70).
7. **Rear body, halo, airbox** (71–95): the airbox (red) sits on jumpers
   behind the head; the halo is a bar in clips; the rear wing stands on a
   central pillar with tall slope end plates.
8. **Wheels last** (96–99): hub + tyre, pushed on pins.

## 4. Techniques, with the reason for each

| Technique | Parts | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open-centre frame plate as floor | 4656764 | stiff long spine, light, room for the driver's legs |
| Technic holes on the axle lines | 3701, 6541 | wheels on pins: they spin, and sit outside the body |
| Inverted curved slopes as suspension | 13547-type | aero-looking arm that also hides the pin |
| SNOT: brackets, bricks with side studs | 99780/99781, 52107, 87087 | smooth flanks for stickers; shapes built sideways |
| Jumper plates (1×2, one stud) | 3794b / 15573 | half-stud offsets: centre a 1-wide thing on a 4-wide body (airbox, halo, driver) |
| Wedge plates in mirrored pairs | 41769/41770 type | wing planform and sidepod floor in one piece each |
| Curved slopes along the spine | 61678 / 93273 / 6508988 | the engine cover's long fall to the rear wing |
| Separate sub-assemblies on one connection | bar, clips | front wing and sidepods built in the hand, attached at a single point |
| Colour as a system | main / accent / detail | liveries change, structure doesn't |

## 5. What the engine takes from it

* **Grammar** (`f1.ts`): floor and Technic axle lines → tub → sidepods →
  engine cover → nose → front wing → rear wing → cockpit (driver on a jumper,
  halo, airbox) → wheels. Every proportion is a ratio of L, so the same rules
  give a 17-, 21- or 25-stud car.
* **Anchor**: the 77244 proportions above are the F1 family's anchor in the
  cookbook; the learning engine explores around it (fidelity is measured
  against these ratios) and keeps what scores better.
* **Liveries**: team palettes are part of the genome, mirroring the range.

Not copied: stickers, printed parts and exact element positions — the
booklet's images don't give depth precisely enough, and the goal is the
architecture, not a replica.
